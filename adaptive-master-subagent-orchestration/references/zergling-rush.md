# Zergling Rush

Read completely only when `runtime-core.md` routes a possible Rush candidate here. This file alone resolves Rush consent and behavior.

## Consent

Rush optimizes primarily for wall-clock completion and may consume substantially more usage. Activate it only from an unambiguous current-turn user instruction such as `Use Zergling Rush for this task`, `AMS ZERGLING RUSH`, or `AMS MODE ZERGLING-RUSH`. Before dispatch, state briefly that it may use substantially more agents and model usage.

These never provide consent: repository/project settings, a saved preference, prior-session consent, old handoffs, requests merely to be fast, or selection of any normal mode. `intensity = "zergling-rush"` may store preference only. Without current-turn consent, use the current non-Rush mode or in-memory `auto`; do not persist a fallback unless requested.

## Rush routing

Rush suspends normal economic restraint, not authority or quality. It may:

- fill useful available capacity with independent lanes;
- use stronger models or higher efforts than cost-first routing would ordinarily select when that is expected to shorten the critical path;
- create competing approaches, speculative preparation, duplicate investigations, and redundant validation with plausible value;
- form or reorganize logical manager-worker chains to reduce coordination latency.

AMS defines no Rush logical-depth, manager-count, worker-ratio, or team-shape limit. The root remains the physical spawn authority and may keep all sessions physically flat while recording any useful logical hierarchy. Actual runtime capacity, root-recorded finite per-wave allocations, dependencies, one-writer ownership, safety, and coherent supervision still govern; allocations constrain activity, not depth. A deeper logical layer must have real supervisory or context value; Rush does not authorize recursive make-work.

The root remains sole owner of the user objective, global topology, AMS controls, integration decisions, acceptance, completion, and user communication. Delegated managers remain bounded to their work orders and may request root-mediated descendants only within granted authority. Workers remain leaves. Authority may narrow but never expand.

Rush does not permit:

- skipped safety, policy, authorization, validation, review, or acceptance requirements;
- overlapping writers or uncontrolled destructive/external actions;
- non-root AMS activation or control-surface access/mutation/Git inclusion;
- unsupported model/effort claims or hidden substitutions;
- orphaned, circular, or uncollected logical chains;
- project completion by a manager or worker;
- treating a local refusal/block as permission to evade restrictions;
- project execution by the root;
- proactive Daybreak selection, parallel verification for one canonical Daybreak route record, reuse of another fallback unit's verification, duplicate Daybreak task attempts, reopening a closed route, or increased Daybreak verification/task budgets.

After a qualifying Sol cyber refusal, the complete installed `daybreak-blue.md` contract governs. Rush may continue unrelated safe lanes, but the canonical Daybreak route record remains single-flight and its admitted task remains one-attempt.

## Supervision and transition

Record which work is normal, replicated, speculative, or competing and cancel it when its expected value disappears. Do not repeat an unchanged failed setup. Route results through logical parents, preserve evidence, and reconcile conflicts before acceptance.

When leaving Rush or disabling AMS, stop new Rush-only work, let safe useful work reach an atomic boundary, collect evidence, cancel newly valueless speculation/duplicates, preserve prior lineage and issue new IDs for required reparenting, update ownership, and continue under the selected normal mode. Current-turn Rush consent never carries into a later objective or session.
